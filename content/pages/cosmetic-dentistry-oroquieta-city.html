Start with the change you want to see

Patients ask about cosmetic care for many reasons, including tooth color, chips, uneven edges, spacing, old restorations, or the overall balance of a smile. Bring specific concerns to the consultation. A clear goal helps the dentist focus the assessment and explain what may or may not be achievable.

Appearance is only one part of planning. Teeth and gums should be examined first because decay, gum inflammation, cracks, bite concerns, or sensitivity can affect which cosmetic approaches are appropriate.

What can cosmetic dentistry include?

Depending on the examination and services available, a dentist may discuss professional whitening, tooth-colored restorations, reshaping, veneers, crowns, replacement of missing teeth, or referral for other care. Each option has different effects on natural tooth structure, cost, expected maintenance, and longevity.

  • Whitening addresses certain discoloration but does not change the color of fillings, crowns, or veneers.
  • Bonding or tooth-colored restoration may improve selected chips, shape concerns, or damaged areas.
  • Veneers or crowns involve more extensive planning and are not interchangeable.
  • Missing-tooth options depend on oral health, supporting teeth, bone, bite, and personal priorities.

No treatment should promise a perfect or permanent smile. Ask about maintenance and what may need replacement over time.

Cosmetic dentistry questions

Can every stain be removed by whitening?

No. Different stains respond differently, and existing restorations do not whiten like natural teeth. An assessment helps identify realistic choices.

Should I bring a reference photo?

You can. A photo may help explain your preferences, but the final recommendation must fit your own teeth, face, bite, and oral health.

Will cosmetic dental work last forever?

No dental restoration is permanent. Longevity varies with the treatment, habits, bite, oral care, and regular maintenance.
