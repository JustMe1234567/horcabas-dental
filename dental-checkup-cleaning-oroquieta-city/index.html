Why regular dental visits matter

A dental checkup is more than a quick look at your teeth. It is an opportunity to talk about pain, sensitivity, bleeding gums, eating habits, home care, and any changes you have noticed. The dentist can examine your teeth, gums, bite, and other oral tissues, then explain what may need attention.

Professional cleaning can remove deposits that regular brushing may leave behind. Your recommended visit schedule depends on your oral health, medical history, and risk factors. A dentist should advise you on timing rather than relying on one interval for everyone.

What you can expect

01Conversation

Share symptoms, health changes, medicines, and previous dental experiences.

02Examination

The dentist checks your mouth and determines whether other assessment is appropriate.

03Clear next steps

You receive recommendations based on the findings, including preventive care or treatment options.

Cleaning needs vary. Some patients may need routine removal of plaque and calculus, while gum concerns can require a different approach. The team should explain the purpose of any recommended procedure before treatment.

When should you contact a dentist?

Do not wait for a routine date if you develop persistent tooth pain, swelling, a broken tooth, ongoing bleeding, a sore that does not improve, or another change that worries you. Call the clinic and describe what is happening so the team can advise you about scheduling. Severe facial swelling, difficulty breathing, or difficulty swallowing needs urgent medical attention.

Good to know

This page offers general education and does not diagnose a dental condition. Recommendations are made after an in-person assessment.

Supporting your oral health at home

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth regularly.
  • Replace a worn toothbrush or brush head and use a gentle technique.
  • Limit frequent sugary snacks and drinks.
  • Tell your dentist about dry mouth, pregnancy, medicines, smoking, or medical conditions that may affect care.

Checkup and cleaning questions

How often should I have a dental checkup?

The right interval is personal. Your dentist considers current oral health, previous problems, home care, and risk factors before recommending a return date.

Will a cleaning whiten my teeth?

Cleaning may remove some surface stains, but it is not the same as tooth whitening. Ask the dentist about realistic cosmetic options if tooth color is your main concern.

Can I book if I have tooth pain?

Yes. Tell the secretary that you have pain and describe how long it has been present. The clinic can guide you on the appropriate next step and available timing.
